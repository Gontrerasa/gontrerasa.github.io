## About Me

Welcome! I hold a DPhil in Politics from the University of Oxford. I am currently Assistant Professor at the School of Political Science, Universidad Diego Portales (UDP). Previously, I was a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Center for Inter-American Policy & Research (CIPR), Tulane University.

My research is in comparative politics, where I focus on popular politics, the dynamics of representation, expressions of political participation, and their consequences for democracy. Concretely, I study how citizens and parties actually connect in contemporary democracies. Three questions drive my work. First, why do programmatic parties still turn to clientelism, and how do they manage it without party machines? Second, *claim-making*: what do the everyday demands citizens make on public institutions imply for representation? Third, what makes participation unequal — who turns out, who abstains, and who gets a response when they ask something of the state?

I work with a mix of methods — survey and field experiments, statistical analysis of administrative data, and long-term fieldwork based on interviews, participant observation, and shadowing, analyzed through process tracing.

My work has appeared in *Comparative Political Studies*, *Democratization*, *Political Studies*, *Electoral Studies*, *Latin American Politics and Society*, *Journal of Ethnic and Migration Studies*, *Policy Studies*, *Política y Gobierno*, and *Revista de Ciencia Política*. All my articles are on my [research page](https://gontrerasa.github.io/publications/).
